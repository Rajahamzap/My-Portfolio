MY ON-PAGE SEO METHODOLOGY

On-Page SEO Strategy – Optimize for Search Intent, Not Just Keywords

My approach to on-page SEO starts with understanding the intent behind a keyword rather than focusing solely on keyword placement or density.

I structure content around a single primary keyword, supporting secondary keyword variations, and relevant LSI terms to satisfy search intent, build semantic relevance, and establish topical depth.

The objective is to create content that comprehensively answers the primary query, addresses related search intents, demonstrates subject-matter expertise, and provides a better user experience without keyword stuffing.

Keyword strategy: target intent, not just keywords

#Understand the Intent Behind Every Keyword

A keyword is not just a string of words. It is an expression of a user need: someone wants an answer, a solution, a comparison or a product. When content targets a keyword without understanding what the searcher expects to find, it can rank poorly, or fail to satisfy the visitors it does attract.

My approach

  • Analyze the search intent behind every target keyword.
  • Understand what users are actually looking for.
  • Identify the information, solution, or product they expect.
  • Examine search results to understand the dominant content format.
  • Ensure the content fully satisfies the relevant search intent.

Every distinct keyword intent should receive an appropriate answer. I avoid creating content simply to accommodate additional keyword variations. The objective is to satisfy user needs, not to increase keyword frequency.

Read the results before writing

Examining what already ranks shows the dominant content format (a list, a guide, a comparison or a product page) and the questions searchers expect to see answered. I use that to inform the structure of a page, not to copy competitors.

Keyword strategy

#One Primary Keyword Per Page to Maintain a Clear Target

Every page needs a clear target. When one page tries to rank for several unrelated primary keywords, its content loses focus, and different pages on the same website can end up competing for overlapping search intents. A single primary keyword gives the page one central subject and one primary search intent to satisfy.

My methodology

  • Identify one primary keyword that represents the central subject and primary search intent.
  • Ensure the page is primarily optimized around this topic.
  • Avoid assigning multiple unrelated primary keywords to the same page.
  • Map closely related but distinct topics to separate pages where appropriate.
  • Use the primary keyword as the central reference point for the content structure.

The objective is to maintain a clear topical focus and reduce unnecessary competition between pages targeting overlapping search intents.

Keyword strategy

#Use Secondary Keywords to Reinforce the Primary Topic

Secondary keywords are generally variations of the primary keyword: different wording that keeps the same or a closely related meaning and intent. People describe the same need in different ways, and these variations help the page cover the different ways users express it.

Important distinction

Secondary keywords are not a reason to create separate sections for every minor wording variation. I integrate them naturally wherever they fit the content, and I avoid keyword stuffing and unnatural repetition.

Semantic depth

#Use Relevant LSI Terms to Expand Topical Coverage

Where secondary keywords restate the primary topic, LSI keywords and semantically related terms expand beyond it. They introduce related concepts, subtopics, entities and additional user questions, which is what establishes subject-matter depth.

These supporting topics must be relevant to the primary subject and its associated user needs. I do not include unrelated terms merely to increase topical coverage.

A note on the term “LSI”

I use “LSI” as a practical content-planning term for semantically related topics and concepts. It is not a claim that search engines use a specific “LSI keyword” ranking factor.

Content structure

#Structure Content Around Primary and Secondary Search Intent

This is one of the most important parts of my on-page SEO methodology.

#Primary content

Primary content directly addresses the main search intent behind the primary keyword. It must:

  • Appear immediately after the introduction.
  • Provide a direct answer to the main query.
  • Be comprehensive and sufficiently detailed.
  • Address the central topic before introducing supporting information.
  • Use the first H2 to clearly represent the primary search intent.

#Example: “Best Movies on HBO Max”

The primary keyword is “Best Movies on HBO Max”. The first H2 repeats the primary intent, and individual recommendations sit under descriptive H3 headings. Only then does the page move on to secondary topics.

#Secondary content

After satisfying the primary intent, I introduce supporting content guided by relevant LSI terms and related search intents. Each distinct supporting topic gets a dedicated H2 where appropriate.

The purpose of secondary content is to expand topical coverage without distracting from the primary search intent.

Order matters

Primary intent first, supporting intent second. A reader, and a search engine, should see the main query answered before the page moves on to related questions.

Content structure

#Give Every Distinct Supporting Intent Its Own Section

When a supporting LSI term represents a distinct user question or search intent, it should be addressed through a dedicated heading and answered directly. This is the process I follow:

  1. Identify relevant supporting terms.
  2. Understand the intent behind each term.
  3. Group closely related terms where they share the same intent.
  4. Create a dedicated H2 for each distinct supporting topic.
  5. Answer that topic directly and sufficiently.
  6. Avoid unnecessary sections that do not contribute to the main subject.
Illustrative example: what earns its own H2 (primary keyword: Best Medical Billing Companies)

One rule for headings

A dedicated H2 should represent a meaningful topic or intent, not merely a different wording of an existing heading. I do not create separate headings for every minor keyword variation.

Placement framework

#Strategic Primary Keyword Placement

I follow a standard placement framework so the primary keyword is clearly signalled in the locations that matter, using natural language and prioritizing readability.

The primary keyword appears naturally in

  • URL slug
  • Meta title
  • Meta description
  • H1
  • Introduction
  • First H2
  • Conclusion
  • Three to four additional contextual mentions throughout the content

The framework is a guide, not a quota. If the exact keyword does not fit a heading or a sentence naturally, I rephrase rather than force it.

Semantic SEO

#Build Semantic Relevance Beyond Exact-Match Keywords

Semantic SEO is the process of organizing content around related concepts, entities, attributes and contextual relationships, rather than around exact-match keywords alone. It helps establish a clearer understanding of the subject and the context that surrounds it.

My approach includes

  • Identifying important entities related to the primary subject.
  • Including relevant terminology and concepts.
  • Covering supporting topics that explain the main subject.
  • Connecting related concepts naturally within the content.
  • Maintaining contextual relevance across headings and paragraphs.
  • Linking related pages across the website.

Topical depth

#Build Topical Depth Within Every Content Piece

Topical depth should not be limited to the overall website. Each individual article should cover its primary topic comprehensively through:

  • Direct answers to the primary query.
  • Relevant supporting topics.
  • Related concepts and entities.
  • Important subtopics.
  • Supporting statistics.
  • Credible references.
  • Relevant examples.
  • Useful comparisons.

The aim is for a page to become a genuinely useful resource on its subject, rather than a collection of keyword-focused paragraphs.

Website-level authority

#Topical Authority Is Built Across the Website, Not Through Isolated Articles

Topical depth also has to exist at website level. A single strong article rarely establishes authority on its own. Interconnected content clusters that cover a subject from several angles demonstrate broader subject-matter coverage.

My approach

  • Identify the website’s core subject areas.
  • Develop pillar pages around primary topics.
  • Create supporting articles covering related subtopics.
  • Connect relevant articles through contextual internal links.
  • Ensure commercial pages are supported by relevant informational content.
  • Expand content coverage around the website’s main services and business objectives.

A commercial page that stands alone has little context around it. Surrounding it with relevant informational content, and linking the two, shows the breadth of knowledge behind the service being offered.

Trust

#Strengthen E-E-A-T Through Authenticity, Expertise and Transparency

Content should demonstrate genuine expertise and give clear signals about who created it and why users should trust it.

Authentic author profiles

Real people with verifiable expertise.

  • Real author identities
  • Complete biographies
  • Links to genuine professional profiles

Transparency

Readers can see how recommendations are made.

  • Selection criteria
  • Research process
  • Commercial disclosures

Authoritative references

Important claims are backed by credible sources.

  • Industry references
  • Research and statistics
  • Expert commentary

#Authentic Author Profiles

  • Use real author identities.
  • Create complete author biographies.
  • Include relevant professional experience and expertise.
  • Link author profiles to genuine social media accounts and professional platforms.
  • Maintain active, authentic profiles on relevant platforms.
  • Ensure author information is consistent across the website and professional profiles.
What I do not do

I do not create fictional author identities, fabricated credentials or artificial expert profiles.

#Transparency

Readers should understand how information is produced and how recommendations are made. For an article titled “Best Medical Billing Companies”, I include a dedicated section such as How We Selected the Best Medical Billing Companies, which explains the evaluation methodology: the criteria used, the research process, the available evidence and the limitations.

Where relevant, I disclose

  • Research methodology.
  • Selection criteria.
  • Evaluation process.
  • Sources of information.
  • Commercial relationships or affiliate disclosures.

The objective is to help readers understand why a recommendation is being made.

#Authoritative References and Supporting Evidence

  • Credible industry references.
  • Research publications.
  • Authoritative statistics.
  • Relevant expert commentary.
  • Direct quotations from recognized subject-matter experts.
  • Sources that substantiate factual claims.

Reliable supporting evidence strengthens content credibility and helps readers verify important information for themselves.

Internal linking

#Use Internal Linking to Distribute Authority and Strengthen Topic Relationships

Internal links serve both users and search engines. For users they provide the next logical step; for search engines they show how pages relate and which pages matter most.

My approach

  • Connect pillar pages with supporting articles.
  • Link informational content to relevant commercial pages.
  • Use contextual anchor text.
  • Prioritize links from relevant, high-authority pages.
  • Ensure important pages receive sufficient internal links.
  • Make important content accessible through logical site architecture.
Relevance over link counts

I emphasize contextual relevance rather than arbitrary link counts. A link should help a reader move to something genuinely related.

User experience

#Make Content Easy to Read, Navigate and Understand

Even comprehensive content can fail users if it is difficult to read. This is how I format content so people can find what they need.

Short paragraphs

Break long paragraphs into smaller, focused sections to improve readability.

Descriptive subheadings

Use meaningful H2 and H3 headings to help users navigate content and locate specific answers.

Bullet points

Use bullet points to present lists, features, benefits and important information clearly.

Tables

Use tables for comparisons, specifications, pricing information and other information that is easier to understand in a structured format.

Clear language

Avoid unnecessary jargon, repetitive explanations and overly complicated sentences.

Optimization

#Continuously Improve Existing Content

I review and improve published content based on its performance and relevance. Content optimization is an ongoing process rather than a one-time activity.

  • Search intent reassessment.
  • Competitor content analysis.
  • Missing topic identification.
  • Content depth improvements.
  • Outdated information updates.
  • Heading restructuring.
  • Semantic keyword improvements.
  • Internal linking improvements.
  • Removal of unnecessary or repetitive sections.
