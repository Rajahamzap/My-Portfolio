MY GEO & AI SEO METHODOLOGY

GEO & AI SEO – Optimizing for AI Search Visibility and Citations

Search is evolving beyond traditional search engine results pages. Users increasingly rely on AI-powered experiences such as Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity to discover information, compare solutions, and make decisions.

My approach to GEO and AI SEO builds on established SEO principles while adding a stronger focus on entity understanding, content extractability, topical depth, authoritative references, and broader digital authority.

The objective is to make content easier for AI-powered search experiences to understand, retrieve, reference, and potentially cite while maintaining strong traditional organic search performance.

How SEO is evolving for AI search

#The Core SEO Principles Remain, but Search Visibility Has Expanded

The foundations of SEO remain largely consistent. What changes is how information is discovered, interpreted, retrieved and cited.

Foundations that stay the same

  • Search intent.
  • High-quality content.
  • Technical accessibility.
  • Semantic relevance.
  • Topical authority.
  • Trust and credibility.
  • Internal linking.
  • External authority signals.

However, AI-powered search experiences introduce additional ways of evaluating and retrieving information.

GEO builds on SEO

GEO is not a replacement for traditional SEO. It builds on it.

The evolution of off-page SEO and digital authority

Authority Is No Longer Just About Backlinks

Traditionally, Google has used backlinks as an important signal for evaluating website authority and prominence. With the expansion of AI-powered search and the broader digital ecosystem, brand authority and credibility can be influenced by a wider range of external signals and third-party discussions.

#Influencers and Industry Opinion Leaders

  • Relevant influencers discussing products, brands, and services.
  • Industry experts sharing opinions and recommendations.
  • Recognized professionals discussing brands on social media.

#Bloggers and Independent Publishers

  • Websites publishing product reviews.
  • “Best-of” articles.
  • Comparison blogs.
  • Industry-specific recommendations.
  • Independent evaluations of products and services.

#User-Generated Content

Authentic user discussions and experiences across platforms such as Reddit, LinkedIn, YouTube and Quora can contribute to how a brand is understood online.

  • Genuine user reviews.
  • Community discussions.
  • Product comparisons.
  • Recommendations.
  • Questions and answers.
  • First-hand experiences.

These platforms can contribute to a broader online understanding of brands, products and services.

What I am not claiming

I do not claim that every social mention or user-generated post is a direct Google ranking factor. These sources can contribute to brand discovery, reputation, corroboration, and the wider information ecosystem used by search and AI experiences.

#A Comprehensive Off-Page SEO Strategy

Backlinks remain important, but a comprehensive digital authority strategy should also consider relevant editorial coverage, industry publications, authentic user discussions, reviews, expert opinions, and third-party recommendations.

Content structure

#Structure Content So AI Systems Can Understand and Retrieve It

Content should be organized so that important information is easy to identify and extract.

My approach includes

  • Direct-answer introductions.
  • Clear and descriptive headings.
  • Logical content hierarchy.
  • Short, focused paragraphs.
  • Bullet points.
  • Comparison tables.
  • Clearly defined concepts.
  • Supporting examples.
  • Contextually relevant entities.
  • Concise answers followed by detailed explanations.

I avoid unnecessarily long introductions that delay the actual answer.

Direct answers and question-based headings

#Answer the Question Before Expanding the Explanation

Content is easier to retrieve and reuse when it is organized around the natural-language questions users actually ask.

My approach

  • Research how users phrase questions.
  • Identify common informational and commercial queries.
  • Use question-based headings where they accurately represent search intent.
  • Provide a direct answer immediately after the heading.
  • Follow the answer with supporting details, examples, and relevant evidence.
Questions only where they fit

I do not force questions into headings when a descriptive heading is more appropriate.

Primary and secondary search intent for GEO

#Satisfy the Main Query Before Expanding Into Supporting Topics

The content structure I use for traditional on-page SEO also applies to GEO. The complete keyword and content architecture methodology is explained on the On-Page SEO Strategy page.

#Primary content

  • Identify one primary keyword and its main search intent.
  • Answer the primary query immediately after the introduction.
  • Place the main topic in the first H2.
  • Provide a comprehensive answer.
  • Include relevant entities and supporting evidence.

#Secondary content

  • Identify related queries and supporting search intents.
  • Use relevant LSI terms and semantic concepts.
  • Give each distinct supporting intent an appropriate heading.
  • Answer each topic directly.
  • Expand the overall topical coverage without diluting the primary subject.

Query fan-out and topical depth

#Prepare Content for Query Fan-Out Through Comprehensive Topical Coverage

AI systems may break a user’s broader question into multiple related subqueries to gather information from different sources and construct a comprehensive response.

For example, a user asks: “Which virtual medical assistant company is best for a small medical practice?” Related queries could include:

  • What services do virtual medical assistants provide?
  • Which companies offer virtual medical assistant services?
  • How much do virtual medical assistants cost?
  • What tasks can they handle?
  • Are they suitable for small medical practices?
  • What should businesses consider when choosing a provider?

My approach

  1. Identify the primary query.
  2. Research closely related questions.
  3. Map the related questions to supporting topics.
  4. Cover relevant entities and subtopics.
  5. Organize the content into logical sections.
  6. Connect supporting articles through internal links where separate pages are appropriate.
One page does not have to answer everything

I do not suggest that one page must answer every possible related query. Some subtopics deserve separate pages within a broader topical cluster. Topical depth helps build a more comprehensive information resource across the whole cluster.

Entity optimization

#Optimize Around Entities, Not Just Exact-Match Keywords

Entity optimization improves contextual understanding by making it clear what a page is about, which things are connected to it, and how they relate.

My approach includes

  • Identifying relevant brands.
  • Identifying products and services.
  • Including industry terminology.
  • Connecting related concepts.
  • Clarifying relationships between entities.
  • Maintaining consistent brand information.
  • Using relevant contextual references.

Original data and unique statistics

#Create Original Information Worth Referencing and Citing

Original information can make content more useful and potentially more reference-worthy. My approach includes using or developing:

  • Original research.
  • Unique data.
  • Industry surveys.
  • First-party statistics.
  • Comparative analysis.
  • Research-backed insights.
  • Data-driven findings.
  • Useful industry observations.

Content containing original and verifiable information can provide something additional to existing search results, and may attract references from publishers, bloggers, researchers and AI-powered search experiences. Where original data is unavailable, I use credible third-party research and attribute it clearly.

No invented numbers

I never fabricate statistics or present estimated figures as verified research.

Authoritative references

#Support Important Claims With Credible Evidence

  • Authoritative industry references.
  • Research reports.
  • Reliable statistics.
  • Expert commentary.
  • Relevant quotations.
  • Primary sources.
  • Verifiable supporting information.

Factual depth and transparent sourcing strengthen credibility and usefulness. I prioritize sources that are relevant to the subject rather than adding citations merely for quantity.

E-E-A-T and authentic brand representation

#Build Genuine Expertise, Experience and Trust

AI visibility should be supported by genuine brand and author credibility.

Authentic author profiles

  • Real authors
  • Genuine biographies
  • Relevant professional experience
  • Consistent professional identities
  • Authentic social media profiles
  • Active professional presence

Brand transparency

  • Clear information about the company
  • Transparent business descriptions
  • Accurate service information
  • Genuine contact information
  • Clear methodology for recommendations and comparisons

Independent third-party recognition

  • Relevant industry mentions
  • Editorial references
  • Independent reviews
  • Expert recommendations
  • Genuine customer experiences
Authentic evidence, not artificial signals

Credibility should be established through authentic evidence rather than artificial signals.

Structured content and machine readability

#Use Clear Content Structures to Improve Information Accessibility

Structured information is easier to understand, for people and for the systems that process content.

Structure elements and why they matter
ElementWhat I doWhy it helps
HeadingsUse a logical H1, H2 and H3 hierarchyShows how topics relate and where each answer begins
Bullet pointsUse lists to make important information easy to identifyMakes key points quick to scan and extract
TablesUse comparison tables for products, services, features, costs and other structured informationPresents comparable information in a format that is easy to read and extract
Concise paragraphsPresent one main idea per paragraphKeeps each point clear and self-contained

#Structured data

Where appropriate, I use relevant schema markup:

  • Article.
  • Organization.
  • Person.
  • BreadcrumbList.
  • Product or Service schema where applicable.
Structured data is not a guarantee

Structured data can help search engines interpret page information, but it does not guarantee AI citations or enhanced search visibility.

Technical foundations

#Make Content Discoverable and Technically Accessible

Search engines and AI-powered search experiences need access to relevant website content in order to discover and process it, so a technically sound website remains essential.

  • XML sitemap management.
  • Robots.txt configuration.
  • Crawlability.
  • Indexation.
  • Canonicalization.
  • Clean URL structures.
  • Internal linking.
  • Structured data.
  • Mobile usability.
  • Page performance.
Different platforms work differently

I do not assume that all AI platforms use the same crawling or retrieval mechanisms.

Internal linking and topical relationships

#Connect Supporting Content to Build a Stronger Topical Ecosystem

Internal linking supports

  • Topic relationships.
  • Content discovery.
  • Crawlability.
  • Page importance.
  • Internal authority distribution.

I highlight relevant, contextual internal links from established, authoritative pages, because they help search engines and AI systems understand which content matters and how it fits together.

#GEO and AI SEO Workflow

Measuring GEO and AI search performance

#Measure Visibility Beyond Traditional Keyword Rankings

Rankings alone no longer describe visibility. I monitor:

  • Brand mentions in AI-generated answers.
  • Citations and linked references.
  • Visibility across relevant commercial prompts.
  • Visibility across informational prompts.
  • Referral traffic from AI platforms.
  • Leads and conversions originating from AI referrals.
  • Traditional organic search performance.
No guarantees

AI visibility is not guaranteed by implementing any particular optimization technique.
